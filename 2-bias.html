Potential Problems with Sampling

Lesson 2.4: Undercoverage, nonresponse, response bias

The three types of bias

Undercoverage
Some individuals in the population are less likely (or unable) to be chosen because of how the sample is drawn.
Examples: calling landlines only, surveying only homeowners, sampling only from those who signed up for remote learning, only people who attended in person.
Nonresponse
Individuals who were chosen for the sample but can't be reached or refuse to answer.
Examples: don't answer or hang up on a phone call, only 28 of 100 emailed students reply.
Response bias
A problem in the data-gathering instrument or process that leads to inaccurate answers.
Examples: people lie (self-reported answers on sensitive topics), leading or loaded wording, interviewer's appearance (firefighter in uniform).
Nonresponse ≠ voluntary responseNonresponse: people were randomly chosen but didn't respond. Voluntary response: people chose themselves to respond with no random selection. The Gallup teacher survey (random sample of 100, 56 replied) is nonresponse.
Does a bigger sample fix bias?No. A larger sample reduces variability but not bias. Bias is fixed by changing the method (e.g. follow up with non-responders using multiple contact methods; use an SRS; reword questions; anonymize).

Direction of bias: how to write it

  1. Name the bias type.
  2. Explain in context who is missing or what is distorting answers.
  3. State overestimate or underestimate (or "higher/lower than the true value") and why.
Template"This is [type] bias because [group] are/is [less likely to be included / won't answer honestly]. These people tend to [have larger/smaller values], so the sample result will likely overestimate/underestimate the true [parameter in context]."

Worked examples from class

Springfield mayor: average age from a list of homeowners

Undercoverage: non-homeowners are not on the list. Overestimate true mean age because older people are more likely to own homes; younger people are more likely to rent.

Principal emails 100 students, 40 reply, 90% did all homework

Nonresponse (60 did not reply) and possibly response bias (students may lie). Overestimate the true proportion who did all homework: students who did their work are more likely to reply (or claim they did it).

Firefighter in uniform asking about fire department budget cuts

Response bias: people know he is a firefighter and may not admit support for cuts. Underestimate true proportion who support cuts.

Counselor: remote vs in-person learning (3 parts)
  • Random sample only from those who signed up for remote learning → undercoverage (in-person students are excluded).
  • Random sample of 100 emailed, only 28 respond → nonresponse (72 chosen students did not respond; not voluntary response).
  • First 50 students who submit preferences → convenience sample.
Social media survey with a leading question

The question describes negative effects of social media first. This is response bias due to wording. Students will feel pressure to look good, so the survey will likely underestimate the true average hours per week.

Homework 2.4 answers

Q1–Q3
  • 1a: convenience sample, voluntary response sample. 1b: undercoverage, nonresponse, response bias (wording, lying).
  • 2 (Whole Fruits): undercoverage; they surveyed only their own customers, who tend to value organic and pay more, not all millennials. 70% likely overestimates the true percent.
  • 3 (Gallup teachers): nonresponse (random sample, 44 didn't reply). Planning time reported is probably greater than true (teachers with more free time are more likely to respond). A larger sample would not reduce this bias; follow up with the non-responders instead.
Q4–Q7
  • 4 (OoberLift): (1) response bias from the leading wording that primes 5-star ratings; (2) voluntary response / nonresponse, since only app users motivated to answer will respond and unhappy users may skip it. Ratings likely too high.
  • 5 (practice logs): response bias (students self-report and may inflate minutes; teacher requirement is 15 min and they logged 17.8 on average). Sample average likely greater than true. Fix: have parents sign off, or use an anonymous reporting process / direct measurement.
  • 6 MCQ: the teacher only asked employees who attended in person → undercoverage (B).
  • 7 MCQ: reduce nonresponse by offering multiple ways to contact and follow up (C).
